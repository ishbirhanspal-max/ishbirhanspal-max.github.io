Understanding Resize Modes

Stretch to Fit

The image is stretched to exactly fill the target width and height. This may distort the image if the aspect ratios differ but gives you pixel-precise control over both dimensions.

Contain (Keep Aspect Ratio)

The image is scaled proportionally so that it fits inside the target dimensions without cropping. The remaining space is filled with a white background (for JPG) or transparent padding (for PNG). This is the default and most commonly used mode.

Cover (Crop to Fit)

The image is scaled to fill the entire target area, and any overflow is cropped from the center. This ensures your output has exactly the correct pixel dimensions with no empty space — ideal for social media thumbnails.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will resizing reduce image quality?

Scaling down (reducing pixels) has minimal visible quality impact, especially when using the "High (92%)" quality setting. Scaling up (enlarging beyond original size) will result in some softness — this is a fundamental pixel interpolation limitation. We recommend not scaling above 200% of the original resolution.

Can I resize animated GIFs?

Animated GIF resizing requires frame-by-frame processing which can be heavy for complex animations. Currently, the resizer processes the first frame. Full animated GIF support is on our roadmap.

Is batch resizing supported?

Single-file resizing is currently available. Batch mode (resize multiple images at once) is being built and will be released as a free update. Check back soon or subscribe to updates.
