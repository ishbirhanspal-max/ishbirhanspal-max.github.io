Why Is There a File Size Limit on Government Portals?

Indian government portals like UPSC, SSC, bank recruitment boards, passport seva, and university admission systems enforce strict file size limits — typically 20KB to 500KB — for the following reasons:

  • Server Storage Costs: Government IT systems process millions of applications. Smaller files reduce storage and bandwidth bills significantly.
  • Low Bandwidth Compatibility: Many applicants use 2G or 3G mobile internet. Smaller file uploads complete faster and don't time out.
  • Standardised Processing: Uniform file sizes make batch printing, ID card generation, and verification faster for government staff.
  • Database Performance: Storing files in databases (common in older government systems) is faster with smaller blobs.

Common requirements you'll encounter: UPSC photo limit is 40KB, SSC is 50KB, most bank PO portals require 50-100KB, and university admission forms typically allow 100-500KB.

Compress Image for WhatsApp — What Size Works Best?

WhatsApp applies automatic compression to all images sent in chat, often reducing a 3MB photo to around 200-400KB. This automatic compression can make photos look blurry or washed out. Here's how to control it:

Sending as "Document" to Bypass WhatsApp Compression

When you attach a file as a Document instead of a Photo, WhatsApp sends it without any compression — preserving original quality. This is ideal for professional photos, important documents, and screenshots.

Ideal Sizes for WhatsApp

  • WhatsApp Profile Picture: 640×640 pixels, JPG, under 5MB (WhatsApp crops to a circle)
  • WhatsApp Status Photo: 1080×1920 pixels (9:16 aspect ratio), JPG or WebP, under 16MB
  • Chat images (compressed delivery): 1280px wide, JPG at 85% quality — around 200-400KB
  • Business catalogue images: 1024×1024 pixels, JPG, under 5MB

WebP vs JPG vs PNG: Which Format Gets the Smallest File?

The format you choose has a massive impact on file size. Here's the ranking from smallest to largest for the same image:

  1. WebP (Lossy): Best option in 2026. 25–40% smaller than JPG at the same visual quality. Supported by all modern browsers, WhatsApp, Instagram, and most platforms.
  2. JPG/JPEG: The universal standard for photos. Good compression for photographs with complex colour gradients. No transparency support.
  3. PNG: Lossless, so always the largest file. Only use PNG when you need a transparent background (e.g., product photos on white, logos).

Recommendation: If you need to hit a specific target KB for forms or WhatsApp, always choose WebP output in OptiPixel Studio. You'll reach 200KB at a higher quality setting (85%) compared to JPG (which might need 65% quality to hit 200KB, causing visible compression artefacts).

Will compressing reduce the visible quality of my photo?

At quality settings of 75% or above, the difference between the original and compressed image is imperceptible to the human eye at normal viewing sizes. Below 60% quality, you may start to see blocky JPEG artefacts — especially in gradients or sky areas. We recommend staying above 70% for photos that will be displayed on screen.

How do I compress an image to exactly 50KB for a passport application?

Start with WebP format at 80% quality and check the output size. If it's still above 50KB, also reduce the max width to 800px or 600px. For most passport photos (which are already small — typically 35×45mm at 150-300 DPI), this combination results in files well under 50KB while maintaining crisp text and clear facial features required by passport authorities.
